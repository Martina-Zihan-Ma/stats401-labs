Lab 9: 2025 GDP — Choropleth vs. Cartogram

Dataset Description

This visualization uses the 50 largest economies by 2025 nominal GDP. GDP is measured in current-price U.S. dollars, in billions, and is joined to country boundaries with ISO-3 identifiers. Countries outside the supplied Top 50 file are shown as no data, rather than as zero GDP.

Source: IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025 nominal-GDP series. Values may include IMF staff estimates and can be revised.

2025 GDP Choropleth

Color encodes GDP while country shape and geographic position remain unchanged. A logarithmic color scale makes differences among the non-U.S. economies visible despite the strongly skewed distribution.

2025 GDP Cartogram

This Dorling cartogram keeps countries near their geographic locations, but circle area represents GDP. Larger circles therefore indicate larger economies, not larger land area.

Design and Comparison

1. Which countries are most visually prominent in each map?

Answer: The United States and China are most prominent in both views. Germany, Japan, India, the United Kingdom, and France also stand out.

2. Which countries become much larger or smaller in the cartogram?

Answer: Japan, the United Kingdom, Italy, South Korea, the Netherlands, and Singapore become much more visible because their GDP is large relative to their land area.

3. What geographic information does the choropleth preserve?

Answer: It preserves country shape, coastlines, borders, adjacency, and geographic position, making regional patterns easy to recognize.

4. What geographic information does the cartogram distort?

Answer: It distorts original country shape, separation, and relative land area because circles are resized and moved to avoid overlap.

5. What tasks can the choropleth best support?

Answer: The choropleth is best for locating high-GDP economies, comparing broad regional patterns, and relating GDP to neighboring countries.

6. What tasks can the cartogram best support?

Answer: The cartogram is best for comparing economic magnitude, because a larger visual area immediately signals a larger GDP.

7. How does changing from color encoding to area encoding change your perception of global economic size?

Answer: Area emphasizes the concentration of global output: the United States and China occupy much more visual space, while smaller economies recede. Neither map is universally better; the choice depends on whether geographic context or quantitative comparison is more important.
