Lab 2: Multivariate Visualization with D3

This visualization explores the relationship among cities, population, temperature, region and development level.

Design Justification

In this visualization, I mapped Region to four separate quadrants (North, East, West, and South) so that cities from the same region can be easily grouped and compared.
Development level is mapped to vertical position, with High placed above Medium and Low, making its ordinal structure clear. Therefore the position clearly communicates the natural order of this ordinal variable.
Population is represented by circle size because it is ration data that can be compared through its size. Cities with larger populations appear as larger circles.
Temperature is mapped to a diverging color scale centered at 20°C. Temperatures below 20°C are shown in blue, while temperatures above 20°C are shown in red, with stronger colors indicating greater distance from 20°C. It can show the difference in temperature clearly.

These mappings allow viewers to compare cities across multiple dimensions at the same time and notice patterns among region, development level, population, and temperature. Combining color with regional grouping makes it easier to notice whether cities within the same region tend to have similar temperature patterns and how temperatures differ across regions. By comparing the positions and sizes of the circles, people can easily gain the insights into the relationships among population, region and development level.
