Interactive temporal visualization of commercial relationships across 60 days.

Day 1 Day 60

Day Summary

Temporal Network Analysis

1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?

Based on the visual trend, the overall network becomes more connected over time. In the earlier days, there are relatively few links and many companies remain isolated. As the animation progresses, more links appear between companies, so the network gradually becomes denser and more interconnected toward the end of the 60-day period.

2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?

Harbor Shipping appears to become increasingly central over time. In the later part of the animation, it is connected to more companies and has one of the largest numbers of links. Visually, it seems to play a more important role in connecting different parts of the network as time progresses.

3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?

Yes. This is especially noticeable in the early period. For example, on Day 1, there are only three links, so most companies are isolated and the network is highly fragmented. Another clear example appears around Day 49, when the network seems to form two separate groups, with Cedar Retail and Ion Systems acting as the centers of two different clusters, while several other companies remain isolated.

4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?

Most commercial relationships appear to be relatively balanced, and there is no single relationship that visually becomes much stronger than all the others. Links appear and disappear throughout the animation, but their overall strengths remain fairly similar. One repeated pattern I noticed is that Ion Systems frequently has a purple link, which represents a service-type transaction, suggesting that service relationships involving Ion Systems occur relatively consistently over time.

5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?

There does not appear to be a strong visual trend toward either more or less cross-regional interaction over time. Region is encoded using the node border color, but this visual channel is less prominent than node fill color or link color, so changes in cross-regional relationships are not immediately obvious. From the visualization alone, the level of cross-regional connectivity seems to fluctuate rather than showing a clear increasing or decreasing pattern.
